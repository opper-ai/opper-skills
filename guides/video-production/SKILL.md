> **A guide, not an Opper API skill.** It shares how we make videos with open-source tools (HyperFrames, GSAP, FFmpeg) and Opper for the media; the method and tool choices are suggestions. For Opper itself, start at [`opper`](https://skills.opper.ai/); request fields live in [`opper-multimodal`](https://skills.opper.ai/opper-multimodal/SKILL.md) and `https://api.opper.ai/v3/openapi.yaml`.

# Video production with open-source tools and Opper

**Generate what must be organic, code what must be exact, measure before you call it done.** Opper gives you every medium and the review senses through one key. Open-source tools on your machine do the rest: the picture is an HTML page animated with GSAP and rendered frame by frame by HyperFrames; FFmpeg trims, mixes, masters, muxes and measures.

## How the work is done

A film here is three layers, each with its own tool:

1. **Media from Opper.** Stills, video clips, voice, music, sound effects and transcripts are generated by small scripts that call Opper and save files. Nothing is placed or timed yet.
2. **The picture is a web page.** Layout, type, logos, UI, diagrams and every animation are HTML, CSS and SVG, moved by one GSAP timeline. Generated clips and stills sit on the page as ordinary `<video>` and `<img>` elements. HyperFrames turns the page into video by seeking the timeline to each frame and capturing it.
3. **Sound and delivery are FFmpeg.** It prepares the clips, mixes and masters the audio, joins picture and sound into the final file, and measures the result.

One cue file (`cues.json`) ties the layers together: the page and the mix both read their times from it, so a retime is one edit. One command per film runs the chain (prepare, mix, render, mux, check) and writes a new, numbered export.

### What the tools can do

| Tool | Capabilities | Limits and habits |
|---|---|---|
| **[HyperFrames](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes)** (open source, HeyGen) | Renders an HTML composition to MP4 or WebM, deterministically, frame by frame in headless Chrome. Timed clips, images and nested compositions via `data-start` / `data-duration`. A linter for common mistakes, a preview studio to scrub the timeline, key-frame snapshots, beat detection from a music file, and website capture for product footage | No audio mixing. 3D only through Three.js driven by the timeline. Strict linter. A 0.x release: pin the version |
| **[GSAP](https://gsap.com)** | The animation engine inside the page: tweens, eases, timelines, SVG and filter animation. A paused timeline is what HyperFrames seeks | Every value must be a function of time: no timers, `Date.now()` or unseeded randomness. One owner per property |
| **[FFmpeg](https://ffmpeg.org)** | Trims, rescales and re-times generated clips to the film's size and frame rate; filters and EQs sound; loudness normalisation to a target (for example -14 LUFS) and limiting; joins the silent render with the mix; encodes for delivery; pulls frames, contact sheets and loudness readings for review | A command line tool: keep the recipes in the film's scripts, not in your head |
| **Python: numpy, soundfile, [librosa](https://librosa.org)** | The mix as code: each effect placed at its cue time with a gain, music ducked or cut with gain curves. librosa measures tempo, beats, onsets and loudness per second, which is how scores are chosen and effects are checked | Decode and encode through FFmpeg |
| **[Playwright](https://playwright.dev)** | Pixel-exact captures of your real site and app, for explainers that must show the true product | Capture public or demo data only |

Other renderers fit other teams: [Remotion](https://www.remotion.dev) for React (check its company licence), [Motion Canvas](https://motioncanvas.io) and [Manim](https://www.manim.community) for diagram and maths explainers, [Blender](https://www.blender.org) for real 3D. The rules below hold for any of them.

## 1. Ask, then plan

Open with a short round of questions unless the user said to just go: purpose and viewer, length and aspect ratio, the look (hand-drawn, flat vector, paper cut, photoreal, motion graphics), whether generated video clips are wanted at all or code animation is enough, narration / music / sound design, allowed brand assets and claims, and whether real people appear (and have agreed). Offer two or three concrete directions with a recommendation.

Then write:
- **`BRIEF.md`**: viewer, the one takeaway, length, look, claims you may make. For an explainer, a **`FACTS.md`** with a source for every on-screen claim, and a list of words to avoid.
- **A shot table**: time, on screen, purpose of the beat, carried object, audio cue.
- **One `cues.json`** that picture *and* sound both read: tempo, every cut, every hit, every narration line. Retiming is then one edit.

Choose the medium by what must be true on screen:

| On screen | Make it with |
|---|---|
| Text, logos, UI, code, numbers, diagrams, anything a viewer reads | Code (never a generator: it garbles letters) |
| Actions that must line up with sound (contacts, hits, clicks) | Code animation. Generated clips invent their own timing and props, and the sound can only guess |
| People, places, weather, organic performance | Generated stills, animated with image-to-video |
| Product truth (your site, your app, a real API response) | Real captures (Playwright) and real calls |

A stylised look (flat vector, line art, paper cut) beats generic realism: it hides model artefacts and cuts well with code.

## 2. Opper, medium by medium

One key (`Authorization: Bearer $OPPER_API_KEY`), base `https://api.opper.ai`. **For request fields, response shapes, async jobs and model discovery, load [`opper-multimodal`](https://skills.opper.ai/opper-multimodal/SKILL.md);** this section keeps only what matters for making a video. Every surface has a model list; read it, never hardcode ids from memory, and test one call before a batch.

| Medium | Call | Models |
|---|---|---|
| Image / edit | `POST /v3/images` | `GET /v3/images/models` |
| Video clip (async) | `POST /v3/videos`, poll `status_url` | `GET /v3/videos/models` |
| Voice | `POST /v3/audio/speech` | `GET /v3/audio/voices?model=<model>` for its voices |
| Music, sound effects | `POST /v3/audio/generations` | `GET /v3/audio/models?type=music` / `?type=sound` |
| Transcription (captions) | `POST /v3/audio/transcriptions` | `GET /v3/audio/models?type=stt` |
| Text, vision, listening | `POST /v3/compat/chat/completions` (OpenAI shape) | `GET /v3/models` |

Every response carries `usage.cost`. Log it with the prompt, model and id in a `manifest.json` per folder, skip files that already exist, and never overwrite a named export. Send `store: false` for throwaway variants. Provider-specific fields go in `parameters` and pass through unchanged.

What we recommend in 2026-09 (check the lists; newer may exist): `openai/gpt-image-2.5-sunburst` and `openai/gpt-image-2.5-flare` first for stills and edits (then `gemini/gemini-3-pro-image-preview`), `gemini-omni-1.1-flash`, `wan3.0` (or `wan3.0-prime`), `seedance-2.5` and `kling-3.0-pro-i2v` for clips (bare names are pooled across providers; prefix one, like `fal/kling-3.0-pro-i2v`, to pin it), `elevenlabs/eleven_v4` for voice, `elevenlabs/music_v2_5` for scores, `elevenlabs/eleven_text_to_sound_v2` for effects, `elevenlabs/scribe_v2` for captions, `gemini/gemini-3.1-pro-preview` to look and listen.

### Images

```json
{"model": "...", "prompt": "...", "image": "data:image/png;base64,...", "reference_images": ["data:..."], "quality": "high", "store": false}
```
- `image` = the picture to edit; `reference_images` = identity or style to keep.
- **Fix a still by editing it, not by regenerating.** "Same drawing, identical in every line; the only change: ...". Regenerating from references snaps back to their composition.
- **Colours as hex or light, never as objects.** "A peach sphere" came back as a fruit with a stem. Write "a round ball of light, coral #F9B58C core, cyan #8CECF2 glow".
- Probe the size you got. Resolution can hide behind another knob (`quality: "high"` gave 2K on Gemini image models).
- Photo to drawing: edit the real photo ("redraw this exact photograph as pencil line art, same composition, same people") so a code wipe from photo to drawing registers.

### Video clips

```json
{"model": "...", "prompt": "motion, then: keep the exact art style, no new people, no text", "image": "data:...", "seconds": 5, "store": false}
```
- Animate a still you already approved. Append each submission id to `submitted.jsonl` **before** polling, and resume from it after a crash instead of paying again.
- Plan to use the first good 1 to 2.5 s; clips drift after that. Cut on the beat.
- Watch for what sampled frames hide: a cut to a new angle mid-clip, invented people, props turning into other objects, a light that tints every shirt. Check a 4 to 5 fps strip and crop the key prop at full size.
- A clip that continues a still must continue its camera move, or the handover pops.
- **Handing a generated subject to code** (an object flying out of the clip): measure its last position from pixels, edit the last frame to remove it, check the two register, then animate a code-drawn copy from exactly that spot.

### Voice

```json
{"model": "...", "input": "One full sentence.", "voice": "<voice id>", "format": "mp3"}
```
- **One file per line**, placed on the cue file. You control the gaps and a bad line is a cheap retake.
- Write warm, full sentences; punctuation is the direction. Agents default to clipped fragments and piles of numbers.
- Pick the voice by ear: `GET /v3/audio/voices?model=<model>` (filter by `language`, `gender`) lists each voice with its accent and, on ElevenLabs, a `preview_url`. Shortlist two or three, then render the same real line in each before choosing.
- The audio comes back in `audio.b64_json`.

### Music

```json
{"model": "...", "sections": [
  {"duration_ms": 8000, "prompt": "120 BPM, C major, playful, sound from the first beat", "styles": ["pizzicato", "marimba", "glockenspiel", "instrumental"]},
  {"duration_ms": 8000, "prompt": "the same instruments, fuller, ending on a resolved chord", "styles": ["pizzicato", "marimba", "glockenspiel", "instrumental"]}]}
```
- Without a timed plan, send `prompt` + `duration_seconds`. With `sections`, the sections set the length and carry the prompt; some models refuse a top-level `prompt` or `instrumental` alongside them (put "instrumental" in `styles`).
- Ask for structure: a round tempo (120 BPM gives a clean grid), key, instruments, "sound from the first beat", "resolved final chord, then ring-out". Never name artists.
- **One instrument palette in every section; vary intensity, not instruments.** Changing instruments between sections gives an abrupt genre switch. This was the single biggest music improvement.
- Describe each section in `prompt` (and `styles`); words to be sung go only in `lyrics`. Put section boundaries on the phrase grid (at 120 BPM, multiples of 2 or 4 s); other boundaries get moved.
- **Do breakdowns, drops and silences in the mix,** not in the prompt. Models often ignore "drums out here".
- Generate 3 or 4, measure all (beats, loudness per second), listen to the survivors, and cue the picture to the measured track.
- Send a `seed` with your `sections` and note it next to each take, so the one you choose can be rendered again. A changed section can still change the whole track, so measure any retake again. On ElevenLabs a seed works only with sections. Sound effects take no seed: generate a few takes and pick one.
- Timbre is the weak point: generated orchestral strings and piano often sound synthetic. Light, plucked and electronic palettes hold up better.

### Sound effects

```json
{"model": "...", "prompt": "a small wooden domino tipping onto wood, one click, close, dry", "duration_seconds": 0.6}
```
- **One action per request, described as the material on screen.** Add `loop: true` for ambience and hums.
- Place each effect on the **measured** action frame (motion or brightness peak), not a guess.
- Normalise every effect to about -1 dBFS peak before gains; some come back nearly silent. Trim leading silence.

### Transcription and captions

Transcribe the **final mix** for word timings. Pass brand names as key terms (`parameters: {"keyterms": ["Opper"]}`; without it "Opper" became "OPA"). Take the words and punctuation from the script, and only the timings from the transcript.

### Looking and listening

Send frames (`image_url`) or the mix (`input_audio`) to a chat model and ask for a blunt score. Useful as a second opinion on "is this good". Never for "when" (timestamps are seconds off), and not as proof: a listening model rated a mix 10/10 with "every effect audible" while measurement showed effects 10 to 20 dB under the music, and vision critics invented objects. Measurement and a fresh critic decide.

### Films made mostly of generated footage

Cinematic brand films and multi-style montages (each scene in a different art style) have their own method: canon
blocks and a hero frame, identity sheets instead of real photos, last frames made by editing the first, clip prompts
written as timed scripts and read before any spend, a model per shot from a shoot-out, events measured from pixels and
retimed onto beats, motivated transitions that reveal the next scene from its own first frame, a code assist for the
key moment, songs with lyrics and karaoke subtitles. **Read [references/generated-films.md](references/generated-films.md)**
before planning one.

## 3. Assembly

Any code renderer works if **every frame is a pure function of time**: frame *n* renders at *n / fps* after any seek. No free-running timers, `Date.now()` or unseeded randomness.

- **Bake every change.** If you precompute state per frame and skip "unchanged" values, use a tiny threshold. A 0.02 threshold froze a slow zoom, which then jumped 40 px twice.
- **Pre-roll every clip** (decode it about 0.2 s early, hidden) and switch hard cuts a hair early, or the cut frame is blank.
- One owner per animated property; move with transforms; bind every font to a local file; frame 0 is a finished picture (it is the thumbnail).
- Draw the logo from the official SVG paths, in brand colours; generate the wall, code the sign.
- Motion that reads as crafted: gravity arcs, anticipation, squash and stretch, follow-through; nothing passes through anything; energy never increases after a bounce.
- Match source frame rates, or motion judders (24 fps clips stretched to 30 repeat every fifth frame).

## 4. Sound

- **Audio first:** lock and measure the score, write beats and hits into `cues.json`, then animate to them.
- Duck the music 8 to 12 dB around each action and 10 to 12 LU under narration.
- **Measure effect lift:** compare the mix with a music-only render in the 80 ms after each cue (1.5 to 8 kHz band). Under a few dB is inaudible, whatever a listener model says.
- Master with two-pass loudness normalisation to -14 LUFS, then a limiter at about -1.5 dBTP. Always export a music-only stem.

## 5. Review loop

1. **Checks on every render:** loudness and true peak; frames that differ sharply from both neighbours (glitches, a layer one frame early); near-frozen time; single-colour frames; cut and hit sync within 1 to 2 frames.
2. **Look yourself:** contact sheets at 2 to 4 fps plus every frame of each transition.
3. **A fresh critic each round:** a new agent with only the render, the brief and the facts, returning ranked defects and SHIP or ONE MORE PASS. It catches what the builder cannot: blank cut frames, a fruit for an orb, a highlight on the wrong code line, an invented failover shown as "a real call".
4. **A human watches last.** They catch what nobody measured (a camera that "jumps", tools whose sounds do not match). Measured is not judged; say which is which.

## 6. Delivery and honesty

- Export H.264 High, yuv420p, constant frame rate, AAC 48 kHz, `+faststart`, trimmed to the timeline. A captioned and a clean version, plus an `.srt`. Vertical needs its own layout, not a crop.
- On-screen claims come only from the facts file. Label illustrative data as illustrative; never put a mock next to the word "real".
- Real people need consent; keep their photos out of shared repos. Only say "made with Opper" for what went through Opper.
